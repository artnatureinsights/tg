3 Insights Into Free Bulgarian Street Puppetry Shows

with Fireter and Yaroslava Bykova Theatre


Did you know Bulgaria boasts the smallest and largest puppets in Europe? Learn about incredible Bulgarian puppets and performances they create from famous puppet makers Yaroslava Bykova (Plovdiv) and Plamen Ivanov, Fireter (Sofia).



Martenitsa: a favorite Bulgarian puppet


World Puppetry Day on March 21 is a great occasion to explore one of the most salient parts of Bulgarian culture – puppet-making.


Pagan dolls and their links to nature feature many Bulgarian traditions.


The pandemic travel restrictions and the quest for safe pastimes have triggered a new surge of interest in puppet performances.


Gradually, marionettes have become beloved entertainers in street shows across Bulgaria.


With 100 years of puppets-making history, Bulgaria boasts rich traditions.


Which stories tell the unique performers during street shows?


To understand this, let's explore the key milestones of the Bulgarian puppets-making history.


Martenitsa is the oldest and most famous Bulgarian puppet featured on the UNESCO Intangible Heritage list. It has the Thracian or Hellenic origin.


The first mention of its use was in the 7th century.


Millions of Bulgarians every year on March 1 create red and white bracelets with or without small puppets - martenitsas.


A tradition maintains that this mascot can attract kind spring spirits and bring all good things to life.


For the whole nation, martenitsa making is the favourite pagan tradition.


In February, workshops in Bulgaria become the busy creative platforms for martenitsa- and puppet-making.


Theatrical puppet performances are part of that festive season.
